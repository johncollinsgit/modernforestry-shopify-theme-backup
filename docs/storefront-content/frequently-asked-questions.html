MY CANDLE HAS NO SCENT! or  WHY CAN'T I SMELL MY CANDLE?!

For the best fragrance experience, let your candle burn long enough for the wax to melt all the way to the edges — usually about 1–2 hours for larger candles (or 30–45 minutes for smaller ones). That’s when the scent really fills the room!

If your candle was freshly poured, the fragrance will continue to strengthen as it cures over the next week or two. Once it’s had time to rest, you’ll notice a richer scent both when it’s burning and when it’s unlit.

ARE YOUR CANDLES SCENTED USING ONLY ESSENTIAL OILS?

We've tested with straight essential oils in candles and have found that they are just not compatible. Through our testing we have found that they have a good cold throw before you light them, but once they start to burn you will either get very little scent throw or a fuel-like smell. Not pleasant at all! By infusing the essential oils into a high-quality, phthalate-free fragrance carrier, the scent throw is perfectly balanced bringing the beautiful aroma created in our candles.

WHAT BRAND OF ESSENTIAL OILS DO YOU USE?

Our high-quality fragrance oils come premixed with the essential oils infused into the fragrance carrier to ensure that they’ll burn well. They are not doterra or Young Living; they are specially made for soy wax.

ARE THESE CANDLES 100% SOY? 

Absolutely. Did you know that a candle can be called a "soy candle" even if it has just 1% soy wax in it? At Modern Forestry we don't blend our soy. It's 100% American-grown soy with no additives or paraffin fillers. 

WHY IS MY CANDLE TURNING WEIRD COLORS?

100% soy candles have no additives to protect the candles from UV rays. These rays have a tendency to bring out the natural colors of the fragrance oils. It does not affect the scent or chemical makeup of the candle, it just makes them look a little different. We recommend keeping the candles out of direct sunlight in order to slow down this process. (We view discoloration as fun proof that we don’t put additives in our candles!)

HOW LONG WILL MY CANDLE BURN?

With a higher melting temperature, soy candles last 30-50% longer than traditional paraffin candles. The 4oz will burn for 20 hrs, the 8oz for 40 hrs, and the 16oz for 80 hrs. These burn times are minimums. Soy wax burns 6-7 hours per 1oz of wax. Duration of wax melts can vary depending on the melter used.

WHY WON’T MY WOOD WICK LIGHT?

No matter what kind of wick you have, you should always remove the top layer of the burnt wick before lighting it. The leftover charred bits of burnt wick can inhibit your candle from burning properly. We recommend using a wick trimmer, but you could also use your fingers or scissors to remove the charred bits. Trim your wood wick down to one fourth of an inch using nail clippers or cutting a small “v” shape in the center of the wick.

WHY WON’T MY CANDLE BURN OUT TO THE EDGES?

When lighting a candle, make sure you have enough time to let the wax melt all the way out to the edges before blowing it out. This usually takes about an hour, but can vary depending on the size and scent of your candle. If you burn your candle for short periods of time repeatedly (not letting it burn to the edges) you’ll find that the candle will tunnel down into itself as it burns. 

WHY IS THE GLASS JAR GETTING BLACK AS I BURN MY CANDLE?

This issue is the opposite of not burning out to the edges. When you burn a candle for too long (max burn time is 4 hours at a time) it will superheat the jar and cause it to turn black. This can also happen if you do not trim the wick. A little bit of soot is a natural byproduct of any flame, but given the correct burning conditions soot can be kept to a minimum. If the jar does get black, the soot can easily be removed with a paper towel once the candle has been extinguished.  

DO YOUR CANDLES SMOKE?

When you blow them out they do smoke, but they don’t smoke like petroleum. Smoke is a natural byproduct of fire, but we have found that our candles rarely smoke while burning. If you burn a candle too long without trimming the wick it is significantly more likely to smoke both while burning and once you’ve put it out. If you extinguish the candle and feel like it just won’t quit smoking, your wick is probably too long. Give the wick a quick trim and that should resolve the issue.
